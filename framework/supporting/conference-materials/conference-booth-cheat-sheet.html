Open Source Accessibility Framework

Make accessibility part of how your project works, one practical step at a time.

30-second pitch

A phased framework for open source projects that want to improve accessibility but don't know where to start. You work through practical actions in four phases: foundational, workflow, testing, and community. You register your project and get a public tracking issue, so users and contributors can see your progress. It's not a certification, score, or ranking. It's steady, visible improvement.

Four phases

  • Foundational: make your commitment visible and establish a way to track accessibility work.
  • Workflow: build accessibility into docs, design, triage, pull requests, ownership, and dependency decisions.
  • Testing: add repeatable automated and manual checks.
  • Community: make collaboration accessible, respond to feedback, share progress, and invite people to help.

How much effort?

Actions and typical effort per phase
Phase Actions Effort
Foundational 2 Both quick
Workflow 10 Quick to extended
Testing 6 Moderate to extended
Community 6 Quick to extended

Quick = under 1 hour; Moderate = 1-3 hours; Extended = over 3 hours. Be honest: the full framework is sustained work, not a weekend project.

How to start

  1. Register the project with the issue form (name, repo URL, optional site URL, maintainer or contributor GitHub handles). The tracking issue is public.
  2. Foundational phase: add an ACCESSIBILITY.md and create an accessibility label.
  3. Other phases in the order that fits; update your tracking issue as you go.

Why adopt it?

  • Clear, achievable steps, not an overwhelming checklist.
  • Builds on existing practices (labels, PR templates, triage).
  • Gives contributors well-defined ways to help.
  • Progress and barriers are visible to users.
  • Community: share experience, mentorship, ask for help.

Common questions

Is it a certification?
No. No score or ranking. It shows commitment to improving, not that a project is fully accessible.
Cost?
It's open source and runs on GitHub. Don't speak to funding or services.
We're tiny / no expertise.
Start with two quick Foundational actions. Each action explains why it matters and the steps. Registering connects you with other projects.
Must we finish everything?
No. Work incrementally at your own pace.
Why public?
Public accountability builds trust and invites help. It's transparency of effort, not a claim of perfection.
Day one?
Register, add ACCESSIBILITY.md, create the accessibility label.
Can AI help?
Yes: phase skills for AI agents. Output needs review and testing.
I'm not a maintainer.
Find issues labeled accessibility + good first issue or help wanted on participating projects. You can also improve the framework via issue or PR.
What do we get?
Public tracking issue, a spot on the registered projects board, a welcome message with a badge, and community.

Don't

  • Call any project "certified" or "fully accessible."
  • Promise features, timelines, funding, or support.
  • Guess. Say "I'm not sure, here's where to look."
