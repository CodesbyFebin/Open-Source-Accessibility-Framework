# Framework talking points

Use these talking points when introducing the Open Source Accessibility Framework to maintainers, contributors, project teams, community groups, or conference attendees. Adapt the language to your audience and say it in your own words.

The framework supports incremental, public, and trackable accessibility improvement. It is not a certification, score, ranking, or guarantee that a project conforms to an accessibility standard.

## Short introduction

> The Open Source Accessibility Framework gives open source projects a practical way to make accessibility part of how they work. Projects begin with two foundational actions, then adopt workflow, testing, and community practices at a pace that fits their needs. Progress is public so users and contributors can see what is improving and where help is still needed.

## One-sentence version

> Make accessibility part of how your project works, one practical step at a time.

## Why the framework exists

- Maintainers may care about accessibility but not know where to begin.
- Accessibility can feel too large to address as a single project.
- One-time audits do not build accessibility into everyday project decisions.
- Users and contributors need clear ways to report barriers and follow progress.
- Projects benefit from practical actions they can adapt to their size, maturity, and community.

## How the framework works

Each action explains why it matters, provides a measurable definition of done, and recommends practical steps. Projects can complete actions incrementally and adapt the approach to their context.

- **[Foundational](../phases/foundational-phase.md):** make the project's accessibility commitment visible and establish clear ways to report and track accessibility work.
- **[Workflow](../phases/workflow-phase.md):** integrate accessibility into documentation, design, triage, pull requests, ownership, and dependency decisions.
- **[Testing](../phases/testing-phase.md):** establish repeatable automated and manual checks for code, interfaces, documentation, and core user flows.
- **[Community](../phases/community-phase.md):** make collaboration accessible, respond constructively to reports, share progress, and invite people to contribute.

Automated checks and AI-assisted recommendations can support this work, but they do not replace manual testing, accessibility expertise, or feedback from people with disabilities.

## Benefits to highlight

### For maintainers

- Clear next steps instead of an overwhelming checklist
- Practices that fit existing project tools and processes
- Better visibility into accessibility work, ownership, and priorities
- More specific ways to ask contributors and accessibility experts for help

### For contributors

- Well-defined accessibility tasks and acceptance criteria
- Beginner-friendly issues and clear requests for expert help
- Documented expectations for contributions and reviews
- More accessible and respectful ways to participate

### For users

- A clear place to find accessibility information
- A documented way to report barriers
- Visible progress, supported environments, and known limitations
- Opportunities to provide feedback grounded in lived experience

### For project teams and organizations

- A shared vocabulary for discussing accessibility work
- Repeatable practices that can continue as contributors and priorities change
- Public accountability without claiming perfection
- A way to move from accessibility intentions to trackable actions

## How to get started

1. [Create a public issue to track the project's framework progress](https://github.com/open-source-accessibility/Open-Source-Accessibility-Framework/issues/new?template=project-registration.yml).
2. Complete the two Foundational actions:
   - [Create an ACCESSIBILITY.md](../phases/foundational-phase.md#1-create-an-accessibilitymd).
   - [Use an accessibility label to track relevant work](../phases/foundational-phase.md#2-use-an-accessibility-label-to-track-relevant-work).
3. Continue through the other phases in the order that best fits the project.
4. Update the public tracking issue as actions are completed and barriers are identified.

## Common questions

### Is this a certification or compliance program?

No. The framework does not provide a certificate, score, ranking, or guarantee of conformance. It helps projects demonstrate a commitment to sustained improvement and public accountability.

### Does a project need to complete everything?

No. Projects work incrementally and can adapt actions to their size, maturity, technology, and community. The framework does not require every phase to be completed before a project can participate.

### Is this realistic for a small project?

Yes. The Foundational phase begins with two Quick actions: creating an `ACCESSIBILITY.md` and using an accessibility label. Projects can continue at a pace that works for their maintainers and contributors.

### What if the project has limited accessibility expertise?

Each action includes context, a definition of done, and recommended steps. Projects can also label issues where expert help is needed and invite feedback from accessibility practitioners and people with disabilities.

### Why is progress public?

Public progress makes the work visible, helps users understand known barriers, and gives contributors concrete ways to help. It communicates transparency of effort, not a claim that the project is fully accessible.

### Can automated tools or AI complete the work?

They can help identify some issues, suggest changes, and support repeatable checks. Their output still needs human review and testing. They cannot reproduce the expertise or lived experience of people with disabilities.

## Helpful framing

### Say

- "The framework supports steady, visible accessibility improvement."
- "Projects can start small and adapt the actions to their needs."
- "Accessibility becomes part of normal project work."
- "Progress and remaining barriers are visible."
- "Automated checks supplement manual testing and community feedback."

### Avoid saying

- A participating project is "certified," "compliant," or "fully accessible."
- Completing the framework guarantees conformance with an accessibility standard.
- Automated testing finds every accessibility barrier.
- AI-generated recommendations replace accessibility expertise or feedback from people with disabilities.
- Every project will complete the framework on the same timeline.
